## Q16. General Science & Technology / Mendel test cross inference

A tall pea plant of unknown genotype is crossed with a dwarf plant tt. Of 200 offspring, 102 are tall and 98 dwarf. Assuming one completely dominant locus, which interpretation best fits the data?

1. The tall parent is likely Tt; a 1:1 phenotype ratio is expected
2. The tall parent is likely Tt; a 3:1 phenotype ratio is expected
3. The tall parent is likely TT; a 3:1 phenotype ratio is expected

**Answer: 1**

4. Requires payment by cheque rather than electronically

**Answer: 1**
A blank cheque figuratively implies unrestricted authority. Negating it signals limits, not the absence of any approval or a literal payment method.

## Q149. Basic English / Passage causation inference

Read: 'After bus frequency increased, average waiting times fell. Passenger numbers also rose, but fares were reduced during the same period.' Which conclusion is best supported?

1. The reduction in waiting time proves fares had no effect on demand
2. Higher frequency alone caused the full rise in passenger numbers
3. Both changes occurred; their separate effects on ridership remain unidentified
4. Lower fares alone caused the full rise in passenger numbers

**Answer: 3**
The passage reports changes but contains a competing change in fares. It supports the observed waiting-time decline without identifying the separate causal contribution to ridership.

## Q150. Basic English / Reported speech tense

Choose the standard reported-speech form of: She said, 'I have completed the survey.'

1. She said that she completed tomorrow's survey.
2. She said that she had completed the survey.
3. She said that I had completed the survey.
4. She said that she has complete the survey.

**Answer: 2**
With a past reporting verb, the present perfect normally backshifts to past perfect, and the pronoun changes to 'she'. The alternatives alter reference, grammar or content.
